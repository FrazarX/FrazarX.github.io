Progression

Every number in the game, and how long each one takes.

Solo Gainz has two ladders and they never touch. The training rank comes from finishing quests: it only goes up, it runs from level 1 to 1000, and it is what the shield beside your name means. The ranked Elo comes from winning PvP matches: it goes down as well as up, and it is what the coloured word in the PvP header means. Nothing you do in one moves the other.

The curve

The whole training ladder is two straight lines.

To leave level n

40 + 10 × n

Level 1 costs 50 XP. Level 999 costs 10,030.

A quest pays, at level n

12 + 3 × n

15 XP at level 1, 3,012 at the ceiling. Harder quests pay proportionally more.

So a level costs

3⅓ quests

Exactly, at every level — 40/12 and 10/3 are the same number, so the pace cannot drift as you climb.

Coins rise far more slowly than XP, on purpose: XP is a ladder and keeps pace with the climb, but coins buy chests at a fixed price — paying them on the same slope would make the shop free by the middle of the game. Rep targets follow a square root for the same reason a linear one fails: a level-500 player owing 250 push-ups is not a workout, it is an afternoon.

The ranked ladder

Everybody starts at 1,000 and nothing is shown until your first settled match. Matches are Elo with K = 24, and matchmaking pairs you with someone near your own rating — so a result is worth about ±12 either way.

Those columns are matches played, from a fresh 1,000. A 50% win rate goes nowhere by design — half the results have to go your way before the rating moves at all, which is what makes it a rating rather than an attendance count. Ratings are only ever settled when both players report and the two reports disagree about who won; a match where both claim the win is void and moves nobody. Leaving a ranked match early hands your opponent the win, and doing it repeatedly locks you out of matchmaking — free the first time, then 15, 60 and 180 minutes, with the count clearing after a quiet day.

The four movement families

A third scale, and the only one that is not a single number: every exercise belongs to Upper Push, Upper Pull, Lower Front or Lower Back, and each keeps its own level from 1 to 99.

Points to reach level n

150 × (n−1) × (n+2)

Level 2 costs 60 reps. The first gem, at level 3, costs 150. Level 99 is about 123,000 points.

What a rep is worth

10 points

A second of a hold is 2, a kilometre is 600 — so one rep is about five seconds of a plank, or a sixtieth of a kilometre.

Gems

8 tiers

Chip 3, Bead 10, Cushion 20, Kite 32, Radiant 46, Brilliant 60, Crown 75, Nova 90. Nothing below level 3.

Points are paid on confirmation and for the whole quest at once — tapping + and − on a counter moves nothing.
